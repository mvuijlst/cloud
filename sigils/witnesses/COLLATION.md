# Collation of ten seals across witnesses

Made 24 October 2026 from the side-by-side sheets in `collation/NN.png` (one row per seal: the sheet crop, then every witness image in `INVENTORY.json` for that spirit). Witnesses, with how they were reached (details and quotations in `SOURCES.md`):

| witness | what it is | reached as |
|---|---|---|
| sheet | `72_Goeta_sigils.png`, a Commons compilation | the object of this study |
| Mathers 1904 | plates of *The Book of the Goetia* (Boleskine 1904), figures 1 to 80 | 100th Monkey Press scan via Commons; plates only 100 ppi, about 105 px per seal |
| Peterson / Sloane 3825 | Peterson's own redrawings of BL Sloane MS 3825 (hand-copied 1972, corrected against microfilm, two seals refined 2025) | esotericarchives.com, 72 figures plus the Bune alternate |
| Harley 6483 | Dr Rudd's Goetia, BL Harley MS 6483 | four seals only, scans reproduced by Peterson (Bael, Agares, Vassago, Bune) |
| Sloane 3648, 2731, 3824 | other BL Lemegeton witnesses; 3824 is the proto-Lemegeton | single seals reproduced by Peterson (Vepar; Amy; Seere, Dantalion, Andromalius) |
| Wellcome MS 3203 | Hockley's mid-19th-century copy ("Five treatises upon magic"), 53 spirits | Wellcome IIIF, full resolution, public domain |

Not reached: the British Library's own images (digitised manuscripts offline after the 2023 attack; Harley 6483 and the three Sloane manuscripts were never digitised), Skinner and Rankine 2007, the Folger's images of V.b.26. Nothing was substituted for them; where a witness is missing the row just has fewer panels.

The ten seals were chosen to span the feature clusters and to maximise witness coverage: 1 Bael, 2 Agares, 26 Bune, 42 Vepar, 50 Furcas, 58 Amy, 61 Zagan, 69 Decarabia, 70 Seere, 71 Dantalion.

## Per seal

| seal | stable across witnesses | drifts between witnesses | what the 1904 plate (and so the sheet) changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Bael | two crossed rods ending in crosses, a hook or curl at the foot, rings at the ends | the central object: Sloane 3825, Harley 6483 and Wellcome 3203 all draw a **globe**, a circle crossed by a horizontal ellipse with a cross on top; stroke weight and the number of rings vary | the globe became a horizontal band of six rings under a chevron. The sheet's Bael is Mathers' reinterpretation, not the manuscript figure |
| 2 Agares | cross on a stem over a bowl of nested arcs, with a horizontal rail at the top | the rail's ends: rings (sheet, Mathers, Sloane 3825), loops (Harley), bare (Wellcome); the number of arcs in the bowl (2 to 4) | little; Mathers added weight and regularised the arcs |
| 26 Bune | two distinct seals exist in the tradition: an organic "animal" (sheet 26, Mathers fig. 30, Sloane 3825 main) and a comb of uprights on a rail with hanging crosses (sheet 26b, Mathers fig. 31, Sloane 3825 alternate, Harley 6483 "Bime") | the organic seal's internal rings, crosses and triangle move between copies; Harley's comb is the simplest version | Mathers kept both, as the sheet does |
| 42 Vepar | a bowl whose rim carries four upright loops, rings along the rim, crosses on stems inside, a hooked tail at the left | Wellcome 3203 draws the whole thing as a **bird** with the loops as plumage: the copyist read the structure zoomorphically; Sloane 3648 matches Sloane 3825 | Mathers' two figures (47, 48) differ only in rings versus plain stems; Peterson refined his 42 in 2025 |
| 50 Furcas | four posts on a sill, rings at the post tops and in the bays, a triangle on a stem, two crosses under the sill: **identical in every witness** | only line weight and ring size | nothing structural; the most stable seal of the ten |
| 58 Amy | sawtooth top edge, stepped lower outline, two crosses, a curl at the right | Sloane 3825 (as redrawn) has a large blot over the middle; Sloane 2731 and Wellcome agree on the stepped outline | Mathers tidied the steps into right angles |
| 61 Zagan | ring at the left, stepped rail, a letter-like group (upright, N-form), a flared cross below and one standing on the rail, a ring hung under it, a cross at the right end | Sloane 3825 and Wellcome 3203 join the middle group to the right bay with a **curved arc**, not a straight rail | Mathers straightened the arc into a rectilinear step; the sheet inherits the straight version |
| 69 Decarabia | saltire between four rings, three crescents, one flared cross in the fourth quadrant: identical structure in all four witnesses | crescent thickness and cross size: Wellcome's crescents are thin C's and its cross is small | Mathers thickened the crescents and enlarged the cross; the sheet's heavy crescents are his |
| 70 Seere | the Lemegeton seal (scalloped double "wing" outline, crosses and rings inside) is stable in Sloane 3825, Mathers (two figures) and Wellcome | the **proto-Lemegeton** Sloane 3824 has a completely different figure, a curved stroke with four hooks: the seal was replaced, not copied, between the two stages of the text | Mathers' two figures differ in the internal crosses only |
| 71 Dantalion | a cluster of rings above a cross, a stepped line, a cross at the left | the right-hand arm: a long curve ending in a hook (sheet, Mathers), a short hook (Sloane 3825), a straight diagonal (Wellcome); Sloane 3824 again has a far simpler figure (a cross with rings and a zigzag tail) | Mathers lengthened the right arm into the sweeping curve |

## What the collation shows

1. **Topology is what copyists preserve; weight, curvature and ring count are what they change.** In nine of ten seals the member graph (which strokes join which) is the same in every Lemegeton witness; in Furcas and Decarabia nothing but line weight differs. That is the empirical content of a "grammar": members and attachments are structural, terminals and curvature are ornamental.
2. **The 1904 plates are an edited witness.** Mathers straightened Zagan's arc, replaced Bael's globe with a band of rings, thickened Decarabia's crescents and lengthened Dantalion's arm. A generator tuned on the sheet alone learns Mathers' hand along with the tradition.
3. **Two seals changed between the proto-Lemegeton and the Lemegeton** (Seere, Dantalion in Sloane 3824 versus 3825), and **two seals coexist for Bune** in Sloane 3825 itself. Variants are not copying noise; they are alternative designs carried by the manuscripts.
4. **The earlier catalogues carry no seals.** Peterson's Weyer page has none and his Lemegeton introduction says Weyer's text has no demonic seals; Scot's Book 15 reproduces only the planetary-angel seals of chapter 7, none attached to the spirit list (checked on the 1665 scan and the 1886 reprint of the 1584 text); Peterson's transcription of Folger V.b.26 marks every drawing with a placeholder and the Offices-of-Spirits section has none. Peterson: the spirit sigils "do not appear in any of the earlier manuscripts of the Offices of Spirits" that he knows, and the sigils of Florence Plut. 89 Sup. 38 "do not match". The seals therefore enter the tradition with the 17th-century Lemegeton compilation, which is also where any claim about their construction has to be made.
