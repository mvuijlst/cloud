All 13 accepted; 13a was the only drawing that needed a fix round. Escapes: 9 in total, all in the two Beleth drawings, all closed lobed outlines (heart, omega, leaf, petal, bulb).

Never needed: `dot`. Needed once: `crescent` (03a crest, 14b fleur), `bez` never (every curve with a role fitted an `arc`, `spiral` or elliptical `arc`). Reached for most: `line` and `ring` by far, then `arc` (elliptical radii did most of the bowl and dome work), `cross`, `tri` (feet, pendants, wedges, flared bar ends), `spiral`, and filled `pline` for the hourglass I-bars (07a, 10a, 14a, 14b). `mirror` was used on 11 of 13 drawings, `repeat` on 6; nesting (mirror inside mirror, repeat inside repeat or mirror) was needed on 07a, 09b and 11a.

Systematic difficulties: (1) `cross` is the member that most often lies: on this sheet a cross frequently has one arm that is a plain stem (04a, 05a), unequal arm lengths (08a) or no second pair of arms at all (04a middle, the I-bars) — a per-arm length/flare on `cross` would remove every wish in this batch except the lobed outline and the S-curl. (2) The renderer draws a requested 4 px stroke as 5 px (OpenCV thickness t gives t+1 px) and `score.py` measures the rendered stroke as 6 px, so every overlay carries a red halo, `iou_d1` is capped around 0.7-0.78 even for a perfect centreline, and `cov`/`prec` are lenient (tolerance 3 px against source centrelines): perfect 1.000 scores hide centreline errors up to ~5 px, which is why I checked every overlay by eye. (3) Worksheet `R` detections also fire on rounded rectangular cells (08a, 10a, 03a), and the `L` list merges collinear edges across gaps; reading the skeleton of the crop along chosen columns and rows was far more reliable than either list for arcs and bowls. (4) The worksheet grid is too coarse for strokes of 2-3 px (13a); a 0.05 grid on an upscaled crop was needed throughout.
