# The arms generator

26 October 2026. Built from [STUDY.md](STUDY.md): a seed gives a coat of arms, drawn as an engraved plate (Petra Sancta hatching, after Rolland) or in flat colour (after the Commons charts), with its blazon in English and an invented name. Browser only, no server code, no libraries.

## Files

| file | what it does |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | the page: one coat per visit (`#1234`), or a plate of sixteen (`#p1234`); engraved or colour, 1483 or c. 1340, svg download |
| `geometry.js` | paths and affine maps, three shield outlines (heater, French after Rolland, round after the Italian page), partition lines, field divisions, ordinaries, arrangements of charges |
| `charges.js` | the drawn charges: geometric ones, plants, objects, and a skeleton for beasts (kept as a fallback) |

| `render.js` | SVG in the two styles; geometry is mapped point by point so hatching keeps its pitch and direction on every charge and quarter |

| `arms-data.js` | generated by `tools/build_web_data.py` from the development coats |

## How a coat is made

1. **Skeleton.** A development coat of the chosen roll is drawn at random, weighted by how often its structure occurs: field division and lines, ordinary (with pattern, number, line), the number, class (lion, eagle, beast, bird, fish, flower, star, cross, geometric, object, plant, horn), arrangement and attitude of its principal charges, charges on and around the ordinary, chief, canton, bordure, label, semy, quartering. 162 distinct skeletons for Zürich (from 484 coats), 493 for Grünenberg (from 1,915). The weight is multiplied by a factor for the skeleton's number of elements (below): 1 for one element, 2.5 for two, 5 for three or four, 4 for five; skeletons of six or more are left out, and quartered skeletons keep their plain weight.
2. **Differencing.** The rolls' coats are simple: counting a divided field, a partition line, an ordinary (and its line or pattern), principal charges (and their number above one, and a charge on them), charges on the ordinary, accompanying charges, a chief (and its charges), a canton, a bordure and a label as one element each, and a quartering as two, 61% of Zürich's coats and 57% of Grünenberg's have one element and 3.9% and 12% have three or more. Simple arms are taken (Or, a lion rampant sable is Flanders), so each skeleton is now differenced, as heralds differenced arms, until it has a target of three elements (60% of coats) or four (40%). Each step adds one element:
   - divide a plain field, as the roll's fields are divided or patterned, its principal charges then counterchanged at the roll's rate (54% in Grünenberg, 58% in Zürich);
   - make one principal charge two, three or more, as often as the roll has each number;
   - set an ordinary between two or three principal charges ("a chevron between three roses");
   - lay charges on the ordinary, or set them between it;
   - add accompanying charges, a principal charge on an empty field, a chief or charges on it, a bordure, a label or a canton.

   The steps' weights are fitted per roll by `tools/fit_differencing.js` and kept in `H.DIFF_W`. The tool repeatedly draws 3,000 differenced coats. After each round it scales every step's weight by how far the share of the element that step adds falls short of, or exceeds, its share among the rolls' own unquartered coats of three elements or more. Both rolls are pooled for this (115 coats), since Zürich alone has 14. Before the fit the weights were set by hand and gave a third of all coats a bordure (table below). Nothing is added twice, and nothing that would crowd the top of the shield: no chief with charges between an ordinary (no roll coat has both) or over charges in the upper part of a field per fess or per chevron, no canton or label with accompanying charges in chief, no label with a bordure or chief, and accompanying charges only on a plain or semy field. Added charges are the small ones heralds used for differencing (mullets, crescents, martlets, roundels, annulets, roses, fleurs-de-lis, escallops, crosses), from the roll's own accompanying and on-ordinary charges.
3. **Charges.** Each charge is redrawn from the roll's library charges of that class in that position (`main|lion`, `on|flower`, `chief|star`…). A charge outside the library in the skeleton (a human figure, a monster) is replaced from the position's whole distribution.
4. **Tinctures.** The field from the roll's field tinctures (or partition pairs, or triples); every element from the roll's tinctures given the tincture it lies on (`main|argent`, `on|gules`, `chief|party`…). For elements of the roll's skeleton the rule of tincture is carried by these tables, not imposed; for elements added in differencing it is imposed (metal on colour, colour on metal, furs either way). A tincture is never laid on itself; an element on a divided field takes neither of its tinctures; a bordure never takes the chief's; a compony or chequy ordinary pairs a metal with a colour, neither of them the field's where the roll allows.
5. **Novelty.** The coat's canonical key is looked up among the development coats of both rolls. If it matches a real coat it is redrawn (tinctures and charges from the next streams) up to six times; a redraw that breaks the rule of tincture is passed over unless the first draw broke it too, so redrawing does not change the breach rate (it doubled it, 2.4% to 4.6%, before this guard). If it still matches, it is kept and the page names who bore it.
6. **Text.** The blazon is written from the tree in field, ordinary, charges, chief, canton, bordure, label order. The name is made as the rolls' place names are made: the determinant of one of the roll's compound names with a generic element in the roll's own spelling (*Hennen-burg*, *Loubens-tain*), never a name in either roll or a place in GeoNames. Grünenberg names take the roll's legend forms at its rates (*Grauff von*, *Her von*, *Frÿ von*, *von*, or a family name in *-er*); Zürich names are bare, as in that roll. [NAMES.md](NAMES.md) gives the method and the checks.

## Held-out material

Drawn by seed 20261026 before any grammar work (`data/holdout.json`). A custodian subagent mapped the held-out regions to edition item numbers without reporting their contents (`data/holdout_items.json`: 38 Zürich items, 39 Grünenberg items, 3 marked uncertain and held out anyway). The custodian found that the e-codices canvases labelled 3r, 3v and 2v are parts of Clemmensen's strip 2, not strip 3; it mapped by what is on each canvas. It also found Clemmensen's page markers shifted by one item around BSB p. 209 and followed the page image. No held-out blazon was read during development; the parser and grammar were fixed before test 2 ran.

What the failures mean:
- **Test 4** measured how close the coats were to the rolls, and the user asked for coats further from them: "more complex but not obscenely complex", since the simple ones are taken. Before differencing it failed almost entirely on one feature, the 21 to 23% of real coats whose principal charge the library does not draw (human figures, monsters, heads, tools), and passed for Zürich on library coats. Now the largest gaps are the ones differencing makes: about one tincture more per coat, more chiefs and fewer plain fields. Against the rolls' own complex coats it now fails narrowly, mostly on the number of tinctures per coat.
- **Test 1** fails on pily fields, dimidiated coats (a charge in one half of a party field), cotised ordinaries, ordinaries of lozenges, and a few others; `data/corpus_structures.json` lists every reason.
- **Test 5, matches** is measured against the 2,584 development coats only. The Clemmensen Ordinary (87,638 medieval coats), Papworth and Rietstap were not indexed, so the true collision rate is higher than measured here; differenced coats are less likely to collide with any of them than the simple coats were.
- **Tests 3 and 6** (readers) have not been run.

## Drawings

The first version drew every pictorial charge itself: beasts on a skeleton (limbs as tapered capsules over joints per attitude) and plants and objects from simple shapes. The user judged them crude next to engraved work, rightly. Every pictorial charge is now an imported drawing (`charges-art.js`, 50 charges, built by `tools/import_art.py` from `tools/art_spec.json`); the geometric charges (roundels, lozenges, mullets, crescents, plain crosses, the escutcheon) and the trimount are still drawn in `charges.js`.

Two searches of Wikimedia Commons, Openclipart, heraldicart.org, Armoria, Heraldicon and DrawShield (84 candidates downloaded, every licence checked on the file's own page) found one consistent hand for most of them: Graham Johnston's drawings for Fox-Davies, *A Complete Guide to Heraldry* (1909), traced by szquirrel (Openclipart), by heraldicart.org (*Book of Traceable Heraldic Art*) and uploaded from there to Commons by RootOfAllLight. Where Johnston drew nothing usable, the drawings come from other engraved sources in the same manner (Guillim, de Bara, Legh, Vinycomb) or from clean flat Commons files (key, arrow, linden leaf, wheel, gonfanon, crown, swan, single wing, fish).

**Licences.** Commons tags the Fox-Davies traces public domain (PD-old); heraldicart.org publishes its collection under CC BY-SA 4.0 while calling the expired originals public domain. Nine drawings are on heraldicart only, and four flat files are CC BY-SA (13 in all). `charges-art.js` is therefore distributed under CC BY-SA 4.0 (which the 2.0 and 3.0 files allow for adaptations), with the credits below, linked from the page; everything else on the page is the site's own. Armoria's beasts were excluded (WappenWiki, CC BY-NC-SA), as were DrawShield's (no per-charge licence) and two files whose licence Commons marks as disputed. No free mount or trimount in this manner was found; that one stays drawn here.

How the drawings are used:
- **Recolouring.** Every path of a drawing is kept in the artist's order as a layer with a role: body (takes the tincture, or its hatching in the engraved style), ink (black lines), white, and claws, tongue or crown where a drawing separates them. The traced files quantise the engraving into grey levels; fills lighter than 50% are body, darker ones ink. On a sable charge, lines drawn over the body turn white (engraved) or grey (colour) so the modelling stays visible.
- **Attitudes.** The set gives one attitude per species, so the grammar names a beast in the attitude its drawing shows: a bear rampant of the rolls is drawn and blazoned passant, horses and unicorns rampant, stags trippant, griffins segreant (`DRAWN_AS` in `grammar.js`). This shifts the rolls' attitudes; the lion keeps both rampant and passant.
- **Crowns.** The drawings have none; for crowned lions and eagles a crown is placed on each head at a position and tilt measured on each drawing (`crown_at`).
- **Words.** The blazon uses the terms for what is drawn: stag trippant, griffin segreant, lion's and boar's heads erased, bull's head caboshed, oak tree fructed and eradicated, barbel naiant, dolphin embowed.
- **Size.** `charges-art.js` is 2.3 MB, loaded once (servers compress it).

## Layout

- **Quarters** are rectangular sub-shields as wide and as high as their quarter, so fields, partitions and ordinaries reach the quarter's edges.
- **Barry, paly, bendy and tierced fields** are built from their partition lines, so a barry nebuly, wavy or embattled has bands of those shapes, and bendy is spread over the shield's own diagonal extent. Nebuly is drawn as alternating bulbous lobes on narrow necks. Divisions drawn only with straight lines (per saltire, gyronny, quarterly, chequy, lozengy) never carry a line in the blazon.
- **Accompanying charges** with no ordinary between them and the principal charge are placed where the roll's blazon puts them (in chief, in base, dexter or sinister chief) or round about it (two flanking, three, four in the corners, an orle), and the principal charge shrinks to make room.
- **Charges on a bend** are spread over the stretch of the bend that lies inside the shield, each wholly inside it.
- **Two or more principal charges with an ordinary** ("three lions & bend" in the rolls' coding) are set between the ordinary, where they can be seen, and blazoned so ("a bend gules between three lions"). A single principal charge stays under an ordinary that lies over it ("overall a bend"), the ordinary then takes a different tincture, and charges on that ordinary are drawn on top of it. Every ordinary has places for one to six charges between it (four for a cross or saltire), each shrunk and drawn in until it lies inside the field; the one in base under a chevron clears the chevron's inner edges.
- **Compony and chequy** pieces are squares as wide as the ordinary (two rows of half squares for chequy). On a chevron they run along each arm from the apex, mitred on the pale line, so the first piece is a whole chevron-shaped piece in the first tincture, as DrawShield draws it.
- **A canton** with a chief stands below the chief. Charges on the field are drawn together toward sinister base, by the least amount that clears the canton, so their arrangement keeps its shape.
- **Within a bordure** the coat is a whole shield of its own. The bordure's inner outline is the shield under a map (scaled to 0.78 of the width and 0.78 of the height, set 11 units in). Field, ordinary, charges, chief, canton and label are drawn through that map, laid out as on a shield without a bordure, and nothing reaches under it. Hatching keeps its pitch, since geometry is mapped point by point. This replaced separate shrink rules for each layout, which had let charges in chief run under the bordure.

## Page

Dark by default, with a light switch (remembered in the browser). Both styles use the same shield, the French one of Rolland's plates. The arms are printed on white paper on either page: the engraved style black ink on white, the colour style flat colour, never reversed.

**Print.** An SVG filter in every coat (and in the svg download) makes it look printed, lightly, far less than the sigil plates: fine noise of about one cycle per shield unit moves edges by up to a sixth of a unit (a hatching line is 0.62 wide); a slow noise about five units across lightens the ink by up to about five per cent in patches; a fine dark speckle of up to five per cent greys the paper here and there. Its seeds follow the coat's seed, so a coat prints the same way each time. `renderSVG(arms, style, { print: false })` leaves it out.
