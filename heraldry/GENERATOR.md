# The arms generator

26 October 2026. Built from [STUDY.md](STUDY.md): a seed gives a coat of arms, drawn as an engraved plate (Petra Sancta hatching, after Rolland) or in flat colour (after the Commons charts), with its blazon in English and an invented name. Browser only, no server code, no libraries.

## Files

| file | what it does |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | the page: one coat per visit (`#1234`), or a plate of sixteen (`#p1234`); engraved or colour, 1483 or c. 1340, svg download |
| `geometry.js` | paths and affine maps, three shield outlines (heater, French after Rolland, round after the Italian page), partition lines, field divisions, ordinaries, arrangements of charges |
| `charges.js` | the drawn charges: geometric ones, plants, objects, and a skeleton for beasts (kept as a fallback) |
| `charges-art.js` | imported heraldic art for the animals, recoloured by role (see "Animals") |
| `render.js` | SVG in the two styles; geometry is mapped point by point so hatching keeps its pitch and direction on every charge and quarter |
| `grammar.js` | the composition grammar, canonical keys, "also borne by", blazon, names |
| `arms-data.js` | generated by `tools/build_web_data.py` from the development coats |
| `test/` | inspection pages: `charges.html` (every charge), `one.html?n=…` (one charge large), `sample.html?s=1&n=16&r=grunenberg&st=engraved` |
| `tools/` | `parse_clemmensen.py`, `parse_structure.py`, `build_web_data.py`, `dump_samples.js`, `acceptance.py`, `import_art.py` |

## How a coat is made

1. **Skeleton.** A development coat of the chosen roll is drawn at random, weighted by how often its structure occurs: field division and lines, ordinary (with pattern, number, line), the number, class (lion, eagle, beast, bird, fish, flower, star, cross, geometric, object, plant, horn), arrangement and attitude of its principal charges, charges on and around the ordinary, chief, canton, bordure, label, semy, quartering. 162 distinct skeletons for Zürich (from 484 coats), 493 for Grünenberg (from 1,915).
2. **Charges.** Each charge is redrawn from the roll's library charges of that class in that position (`main|lion`, `on|flower`, `chief|star`…). A charge outside the library in the skeleton (a human figure, a monster) is replaced from the position's whole distribution.
3. **Tinctures.** The field from the roll's field tinctures (or partition pairs, or triples); every element from the roll's tinctures given the tincture it lies on (`main|argent`, `on|gules`, `chief|party`…). The rule of tincture is carried by these tables, not imposed; a tincture is never laid on itself.
4. **Novelty.** The coat's canonical key is looked up among the development coats of both rolls. If it matches a real coat it is redrawn (tinctures and charges from the next streams) up to six times; a redraw that breaks the rule of tincture is passed over unless the first draw broke it too, so redrawing does not change the breach rate (it doubled it, 2.4% to 4.6%, before this guard). If it still matches, it is kept and the page names who bore it.
5. **Text.** The blazon is written from the tree in field, ordinary, charges, chief, canton, bordure, label order. The name comes from an order-2 letter model over the roll's family names, kept only if it is at least two edits from every name in both rolls and contains none of five letters or more.

Rates reproduced without tuning: quartered coats 1.0% (Zürich; data 1.0%) and 7.3% (Grünenberg; data 7.0%); rule-of-tincture breaches 2.5% (data 2.0 to 2.9%).

## Held-out material

Drawn by seed 20261026 before any grammar work (`data/holdout.json`). A custodian subagent mapped the held-out regions to edition item numbers without reporting their contents (`data/holdout_items.json`: 38 Zürich items, 39 Grünenberg items, 3 marked uncertain and held out anyway). The custodian found that the e-codices canvases labelled 3r, 3v and 2v are parts of Clemmensen's strip 2, not strip 3; it mapped by what is on each canvas. It also found Clemmensen's page markers shifted by one item around BSB p. 209 and followed the page image. No held-out blazon was read during development; the parser and grammar were fixed before test 2 ran.

What the failures mean:
- **Test 4** fails almost entirely on one feature: 21 to 23% of real coats carry a principal charge the library does not draw (human figures, monsters, heads, tools; `largest_mean_gaps.m_other`), which the generator never produces. Where the library does reach, the generator's coats are close to the rolls' by this test (the secondary line: a pass for Zürich, a narrow miss for Grünenberg). The next largest gap is the number of distinct tinctures per coat (generated 2.09, corpus 1.98 to 2.01).
- **Test 5, repetition** fails because the commonest generated coat appears 6 or 7 times in 500 where the rolls' commonest appears 5 to 6.5 times; the gap is within sampling noise but the gate was set without a noise allowance.
- **Test 1** fails on pily fields, dimidiated coats (a charge in one half of a party field), cotised ordinaries, ordinaries of lozenges, and a few others; `data/corpus_structures.json` lists every reason.
- **Test 5, matches** is measured against the 2,584 development coats only. The Clemmensen Ordinary (87,638 medieval coats), Papworth and Rietstap were not indexed, so the true collision rate is higher than 6%.
- **Tests 3 and 6** (readers) have not been run.

## Animals

The first version drew its beasts on a skeleton (`charges.js`, `beast()`): limbs as tapered capsules over joints per attitude, one construction for lion, bear, boar, wolf, talbot, stag, horse, goat, bull, unicorn and griffin in rampant, passant and statant. The user judged them not good enough, rightly.

They are now replaced by free heraldic drawings (`charges-art.js`, 24 charges, built by `tools/import_art.py` from `tools/art_spec.json`). A search of Wikimedia Commons, Openclipart, Armoria, Heraldicon and DrawShield (41 candidates downloaded, every licence checked on the file's own page) found one consistent hand: Graham Johnston's drawings for Fox-Davies, *A Complete Guide to Heraldry* (1909), traced by szquirrel (Openclipart) and RootOfAllLight (Commons), public domain. Armoria's beasts were excluded (WappenWiki, CC BY-NC-SA), as were DrawShield's (no per-charge licence).

How they are used:
- **Recolouring.** Every path of a drawing is kept in the artist's order as a layer with a role: body (takes the tincture, or its hatching in the engraved style), ink (black lines), white, and claws, tongue or crown where a drawing separates them. The traced files quantise the engraving into grey levels; fills lighter than 50% are body, darker ones ink. On a sable charge, lines drawn over the body turn white (engraved) or grey (colour) so the modelling stays visible.
- **Attitudes.** The set gives one attitude per species, so the grammar names a beast in the attitude its drawing shows: a bear rampant of the rolls is drawn and blazoned passant, horses and unicorns rampant, stags trippant, griffins segreant (`DRAWN_AS` in `grammar.js`). This shifts the rolls' attitudes; the lion keeps both rampant and passant.
- **Crowns.** The drawings have none; for crowned lions and eagles a drawn crown is placed on each head at a position recorded per drawing (`crown_at`).
- **Words.** The blazon uses the terms for what is drawn: stag trippant, griffin segreant, lion's and boar's heads erased, bull's head caboshed, martlet volant, oak tree fructed and eradicated.
- **Size.** `charges-art.js` is 1.47 MB, loaded once.

The drawn skeleton remains in `charges.js` as the fallback when `charges-art.js` is absent, and the geometric charges, plants and objects are still drawn here.
